\begin{abstract}
Multimodal models are usually compared by deleting modalities at random from
complete test cases. We ask what such an evaluation, together with the data
an evaluator can actually observe, identifies about the risk difference of
two fixed models under the natural, possibly informative, missingness of
deployment. We write the observable information of four observation models
(an oracle that knows the full-data law; complete cases only; complete cases
plus mask frequencies and the unlabelled law of incomplete units; and the
same conditionals without selection fractions) as linear constraints on the
joint law of cells and masks, and combine them with a pattern-mixture
sensitivity model. For finite supports this yields exact linear programs,
but the paper's claims are about identification, not about the programs. We
show that (i) the general identification criterion is orthogonality of the
loss contrast to the span of feasible differences, and the familiar
additive-separability condition needs a relative-interior assumption that
fails at the MCAR boundary; (ii) with complete cases and unlabelled
incomplete units, the natural-missingness Brier-risk difference is
identified only if the two models agree on every incomplete input; (iii)
without mask frequencies the MCAR model yields at most weak dominance, with
one endpoint reachable only in the closure (no complete cases); and (iv)
a feasible but too small sensitivity parameter can certify the wrong sign.
In one hand-specified binary population (exploratory), random dropout on
complete cases ranks the models opposite to the natural-missingness truth in
a label-dependent environment. In a pre-registered semi-synthetic pilot on a
public census dataset (real covariates and labels, a fixed label-dependent
masking rule), complete-case dropout again disagrees in sign with the
held-out natural-missingness difference ($+0.0006$ vs.\ $-0.0038$), while
every non-empty identified set and every outer confidence interval admits
both orders. Results on real multimodal missingness are not reported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Robustness to missing modalities is typically measured by \emph{artificial}
deletion: a modality is dropped at random from complete test cases and the
resulting risks of competing models are compared
\cite{neverova2016moddrop,ma2022multimodal}. At deployment, however,
modalities are missing for reasons, such as cost, device failure, or a
clinician's judgement, that can depend on the missing values and on the
outcome. The evaluation therefore conflates two different random masks: the
artificial mask $M$, which the evaluator draws independently of the data,
and the natural mask $R$, whose law is unknown. It also usually conflates two
populations: complete cases, on which $M$ is applied, and the full population
in which $R$ occurs. Under informative missingness the complete-case law
$P(C\mid R=\one)$ differs from the full-data law $P(C)$.

This paper studies a narrow question: \emph{given what an evaluator can
observe, what does a missing-modality evaluation identify about the
natural-missingness risk difference of two fixed models?} We do not propose
a new estimator or a new optimisation method. Finite-support partial
identification by linear programming is classical
\cite{manski2003partial,balke1997bounds,horowitz2000nonparametric}, bounded
likelihood-ratio sensitivity models are standard
\cite{rosenbaum1987sensitivity,tan2006distributional,zhao2019sensitivity,dorn2023sharp},
and bounding a performance \emph{difference} directly instead of two separate
risks is known to exploit shared uncertainty \cite{guerdan2024predictive}. Our
contribution is an explicit account of the observation model and of what it
does and does not identify in this evaluation problem:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Observation models.} We separate four information sets
(A: full-data law known, as an oracle; B: complete cases only; C: complete
cases plus mask frequencies and the unlabelled law of incomplete units; D:
the conditionals of C without selection fractions) and derive each
observable as a linear constraint on the joint mass $p(c,r)=P(C=c,R=r)$. We
show that the pattern-mixture sensitivity model we use is exactly linear
after a reparametrisation, and point out where a naive plug-in of unknown
products would \emph{not} be (\cref{sec:setup}).
\item \textbf{Identification limits.} We state the general criterion
(orthogonality to feasible differences), show where additive separability
fails (the MCAR boundary, structural zeros), and prove that in setting C the
Brier-risk difference is identified only when the two models agree on all
incomplete inputs, that in settings B and D its sign is undetermined even
under MCAR, and that deleting a support restriction differs from letting the
sensitivity parameter grow (\cref{sec:ident}).
\item \textbf{Failure modes.} We exhibit an environment in which random
dropout on complete cases ranks the models in the wrong order, and show that
a sensitivity parameter below the truth can yield a non-empty set that
certifies the wrong sign (\cref{sec:counter}).
\item \textbf{Sampling.} Population identified sets are not confidence sets.
We give an outer confidence interval with a short validity proof and show that
the plug-in feasibility of the MCAR model is an unreliable diagnostic
(\cref{sec:sampling}).
\end{itemize}
All numerical results come from one hand-specified finite population with
two binary modalities; they are exact (rational arithmetic with certified
linear programs) but they illustrate the statements rather than estimate how
often rankings change in practice. Results on real multimodal data are not
reported; a pre-registered semi-synthetic pilot with real covariates and
labels but synthetic masks is in \cref{sec:pilot} \todo{P2-real: dataset with
natural modality missingness, license, approval, pre-registered analysis}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Missingness shift.} \citet{zhou2023domain} study domain adaptation
when source and target share $P(X,Y)$ but differ in the missingness
mechanism, with identification results for MCAR-type shifts. The preprint of
\citet{rockenschaub2024robust} shows that the Bayes predictor is invariant
only under ignorable shifts and that label-dependent shifts are not
ignorable. \citet{choi2026icym2i} show that estimates of a modality's value
are biased when modality availability differs between source and target and
correct them by inverse probability weighting under a missing-at-random
assumption. These works learn or re-weight under ignorability; we study
non-ignorable (value- and label-dependent) missingness through partial
identification and do not learn predictors.

\textbf{Evaluation under shift.} \citet{subbaswamy2021evaluating} define the
worst-case risk of a fixed model when a user-chosen conditional $P(W\mid Z)$
shifts, as the expected loss on the worst $(1-\alpha)$ subsample, and
estimate it by a conditional-quantile (CVaR-type) dual. Their sepsis case
study shifts $P(\text{test order}\mid \text{demographics, disease status})$,
i.e.\ a label-dependent measurement policy, and reports the worst-case
accuracy of two models separately. Our setting can be written in their
notation with $W=R$ and $Z=(X,Y)$; we differ by (i) conditioning the
information on what is observable when $X$ is partly missing, (ii) using a
two-sided pattern-mixture ratio instead of a one-sided subsample bound, and
(iii) bounding the difference of two models. \citet{guerdan2024predictive}
bound the performance difference (a ``$\delta$-regret interval'') between a
status-quo decision policy and a proposed one under selective labels, using
marginal-sensitivity-type bounding functions, and show that terms shared by
both policies cancel; their minimal uncertainty set is stated given the
bounding functions and they note that the resulting interval need not be the
tightest implied by the causal assumption. The joint-versus-separate
comparison in our experiments is an instance of their observation and is not
claimed as new.

\textbf{Partial identification and sensitivity analysis.} Sharp bounds with
missing outcomes and covariates go back to Manski
\cite{manski2003partial,horowitz2000nonparametric}; linear-programming bounds
for discrete variables are standard \cite{balke1997bounds}. Our sensitivity
model is a pattern-mixture restriction \cite{little1993pattern} of the
bounded-likelihood-ratio type used by
\citet{rosenbaum1987sensitivity,tan2006distributional,zhao2019sensitivity,dorn2023sharp}
and, for biased samples, by \citet{sahoo2022learning}. The ranking-transfer
threshold we report is a tipping-point summary in the spirit of the E-value
\cite{vanderweele2017sensitivity}. Confidence sets for partially identified
parameters are discussed by \citet{imbens2004confidence,stoye2009more}; our
outer interval is a simple, conservative projection construction.

\textbf{Adversarial deletion and missing labels.} Worst-case per-instance
feature deletion is studied for learning by \citet{globerson2006nightmare}.
Non-random missingness of ratings changes the apparent ranking of
recommender models \cite{marlin2009collaborative}, and robust policy
improvement under confounding is studied by \citet{kallus2018confounding}.
These concern learning or missing labels rather than evaluation with missing
features.

\section{Problem Setup: Observation Models}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Population and masks.} A unit has a cell $c=(x,y)\in\cC$ with
$x\in\{0,1\}^d$ and $y\in\{0,1\}$, a natural mask $R\in\cR=\{0,1\}^d$ ($r_j=1$
means modality $j$ is observed, $\one$ is the complete mask), and a label
indicator $S\in\{0,1\}$. The unknown joint mass is
\[
p(c,r)=P(C=c,R=r),\qquad w_c=\textstyle\sum_r p(c,r),\quad \rho_r=\sum_c p(c,r).
\]
The evaluator's artificial mask $M\sim q$ is drawn independently of $(C,R)$.
Two fixed predictors $f_A,f_B$ see $x_r$ (the observed coordinates) and $r$;
$L_f(c,r)=\ell(f(x_r,r),y)$ and $D(c,r)=L_A(c,r)-L_B(c,r)$. We use the Brier
loss, so $D(c,r)=u(x_r)-2y\,\delta(x_r)$ with $\delta=f_A-f_B$ and
$u=f_A^2-f_B^2$.

\textbf{Estimands.} The target is the natural-missingness risk difference
\[
\Dnat=\textstyle\sum_{c,r}p(c,r)\,D(c,r).
\]
The usual evaluation computes the random-dropout difference on complete
cases, $\Delta^{\mathrm{drop}}_{\mathrm{cc}}(q)=\sum_c v_c\sum_r q_r D(c,r)$
with $v_c=P(C=c\mid R=\one)$. We write
$\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r=\sum_c v_c D(c,r)$ for the per-mask differences on
complete cases.

\begin{assumption}[Labels]\label{ass:labels}
$S=\mathbb{1}[R=\one]$: labels are observed exactly for complete cases. (If
label availability were independent of the mask, $S$ would enter as a
separate factor; we do not analyse that case.)
\end{assumption}

\textbf{Information sets.} Each observable becomes one family of linear rows
in the unknowns. Constants are the observed quantities; unknowns are $p$ and
the auxiliary scalars listed.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{B}] complete cases only (known: $v$; unknown: $p$, $\rho_{\one}$):
$p(c,\one)-\rho_{\one}v_c=0$ for all $c$; $\sum_{c,r}p(c,r)=1$.
\item[\textbf{C}] one sample with \cref{ass:labels} (known: $\theta_c=p(c,\one)$
and $\omega(r,o)=P(R=r,X_r=o)$ for $r\neq\one$):
$p(c,\one)=\theta_c$; $\sum_{c:\,x_r(c)=o}p(c,r)=\omega(r,o)$. These rows fix
all $\rho_r$.
\item[\textbf{D}] separate samples (known: $v$ and
$\mu_r(o)=P(X_r=o\mid R=r)$; unknown: $p,\rho$):
$p(c,\one)-\rho_{\one}v_c=0$; $\sum_{c:\,x_r(c)=o}p(c,r)-\mu_r(o)\rho_r=0$.
\item[\textbf{A}] oracle: the rows of C plus $\sum_r p(c,r)=w_c$ with $w$
known. A is not available in practice; it is the observation model implicitly
assumed when complete data are treated as the population.
\end{itemize}
In the notation above, B $\supseteq$ D $\supseteq$ C $\supseteq$ A as feasible
sets. Replacing $P(C\mid R=\one)$ by $P(C)$ moves the analysis from C to A
without justification; our experiments show the change is material.

\textbf{Sensitivity model.} For each incomplete mask $r$, the full-data law
among units with mask $r$ may differ from the complete-case law by a bounded
likelihood ratio:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mcc}
\frac{\lambda_r}{\Gamma}\le\frac{P(C=c\mid R=r)}{v_c}\le\lambda_r\Gamma
\quad\text{for all }c\text{ with }v_c>0,
\qquad P(C=c\mid R=r)=0\text{ if }v_c=0,
\end{equation}
for some unknown $\lambda_r>0$; equivalently $P(\cdot\mid R=r)$ is absolutely
continuous with respect to the complete-case law and the ratio varies across
cells by at most $\Gamma^2$. (v1 stated the ratio bound only for $v_c>0$ and
omitted the absolute-continuity clause, which the linear program always
imposed.) We write $\Gamma_{cc}$ for this parameter. It is \emph{not} the
parameter $\Gamma_{\mathrm{box}}$ of the earlier oracle analysis
(\cref{app:fr1}), whose reference is the dropout law $q$ (equivalently the
full-data law $w$) with a fixed scale; the two numbers must not be compared. We call this $\Mcc(\Gamma)$. $\Gamma=1$ means every mask
has the complete-case law, i.e.\ the mask is independent of the cell (MCAR).
$\Gamma$ is chosen by the analyst and is not estimable.

\begin{lemma}[Exact linearisation]\label{lem:linear}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:mcc} by $\rho_r$ gives
$\rho_r\lambda_r v_c/\Gamma\le p(c,r)\le\Gamma\rho_r\lambda_r v_c$, which contains
the product of two unknowns. Because $\lambda_r$ appears in no other
constraint, the map $(\rho_r,\lambda_r)\mapsto b_r=\rho_r\lambda_r$ is onto
$[0,\infty)$ whenever $\rho_r>0$, and $p(\cdot,r)=0$ when $\rho_r=0$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mcc-lin}
b_r\kappa_c/\Gamma\le p(c,r)\le\Gamma b_r\kappa_c,\qquad b_r\ge 0
\end{equation}
with the known constant $\kappa=v$ (B, D) or $\kappa=\theta$ (A, C) describes
the same set of $p$ among laws with $\rho_{\one}>0$. No unknown product is
plugged in. Reverse construction: from a feasible $(p,b)$ with $\rho_{\one}>0$,
set $v=p(\cdot,\one)/\rho_{\one}$ and, for masks with $\rho_r>0$,
$\lambda_r=b_r/\rho_r$ (times $\rho_{\one}$ when $\kappa=\theta$); then
\eqref{eq:mcc} holds, including absolute continuity. If $\rho_{\one}=0$ the
point is not a probability model of the data (no complete cases) and is only
a limit of such models.
\end{lemma}

The identified set of $p$ is therefore the projection of a polytope, and
$\Dnat$ ranges over an interval computed by two linear programs.
\Cref{app:proofs} records one caveat: in B and D the programs admit
$\rho_{\one}=0$, which is not a valid population (no complete cases), so
the computed interval is the \emph{closure} of the identified set.

Two relaxations of \eqref{eq:mcc-lin} must be kept apart. \textsc{Support-only}
keeps only $p(c,r)=0$ whenever $\kappa_c=0$; it is the closure of
$\bigcup_{\Gamma<\infty}\Mcc(\Gamma)$. \textsc{No-model} drops all
restrictions (Manski-type). They differ exactly when the complete-case
support misses a cell.

\textbf{Reading an interval.} For $\Delta=R_A-R_B$ with identified interval
$[\ell,u]$ we distinguish: \emph{strict} ($u<0$ or $\ell>0$; margin
$\min(|\ell|,|u|)$); \emph{weak} ($u=0$ or $\ell=0$ and the zero endpoint is
attained by a valid model: one model is never worse and a tie is compatible
with the data); \emph{no-margin} (the zero endpoint is reached only in the
closure: strict for every valid model but without a uniform margin);
\emph{both orders} ($\ell<0<u$; interior values are always attained by valid
models because the valid set is convex); and \emph{tie}. Attainment is
checked by one extra linear program that maximises $\rho_{\one}$ subject to the
objective equal to the endpoint. v1 labelled weak cases as ``undetermined''.

\section{Identification}
\label{sec:ident}

Let $F$ be the lifted feasible polytope in $(p,b,\rho)$ and
$g$ the objective, with zeros on auxiliary coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[General criterion]\label{thm:general}
$\Dnat$ is constant on $F$ if and only if $g\perp\mathrm{span}\{z-z':z,z'\in F\}$.
Equivalently, $g$ lies in the row space of the equality constraints augmented
with $e_j^\top$ for every coordinate $j$ that is constant on $F$.
\end{theorem}
This is a standard polyhedral fact (the affine hull of $F$ is cut out by its
equalities and implicit equalities); we state it because the familiar
row-space test \emph{without} the implicit equalities is only sufficient.
It becomes necessary under a relative-interior assumption: some $z\in F$ lies
strictly inside every non-degenerate bound. That assumption fails in three
cases that matter here. (a) At $\Gamma=1$ every incomplete coordinate is tied
to $b_r$; in our population the explicit test reports ``not identified''
while the width is exactly zero (\cref{tab:audit}). (b) With structural zeros,
where $\kappa_c=0$ forces $p(c,r)=0$. (c) At the smallest $\Gamma$ for which
a set is feasible, bounds may bind at every feasible point.

\begin{proposition}[MCAR boundary]\label{prop:gamma1}
Under $\Mcc(1)$, $P(C\mid R=r)=v$ for every $r$ with $\rho_r>0$, so
$\Dnat=\sum_r\rho_r\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r$.
(i) In C, $\Dnat$ is point-identified, and $\Mcc(1)$ is refutable: it is
feasible iff $\omega(r,o)=\rho_r\sum_{c:x_r(c)=o}v_c$ for all $(r,o)$.
(ii) In B and D, the identified set is
$\{\sum_r\rho_r\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r:\rho\in\Delta(\cR),\rho_{\one}>0\}$, whose
closure is $[\min_r\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r,\max_r\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r]$. Endpoints
that require $\rho_{\one}=0$ are not attained. Both strict orders are
compatible iff the per-mask differences take both strict signs; if they are
all $\le 0$ and $\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_{\one}=0$ (as when the models agree on
complete inputs), A is weakly better: never worse, with a tie attained at
$\rho_{\one}=1$.
(iii) $\Delta^{\mathrm{drop}}_{\mathrm{cc}}(q)=\Dnat$ at $\Gamma=1$ iff
$\sum_r(\rho_r-q_r)\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Setting C: identification requires agreement on incomplete inputs]\label{prop:C}
In setting C, assume $\theta_c>0$ for all $c$ and $\Gamma^2$ strictly larger
than the true cross-cell ratio in \eqref{eq:mcc}, so that the true $p^*$ is
interior. Then $\Dnat$ is identified iff, for every incomplete mask $r$ and
observed value $o$, $D(\cdot,r)$ is constant on the group
$G(r,o)=\{c:x_r(c)=o\}$. Under the Brier loss, if every group contains cells
with both labels, this holds iff $f_A(o,r)=f_B(o,r)$ for every incomplete
input $(o,r)$. In that case $\Dnat=\sum_c\theta_c D(c,\one)$ is a
complete-case quantity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Oracle A]
Adding $\sum_r p(c,r)=w_c$ enlarges the row space to functions
$a(c)+b(r,x_r(c))$ on incomplete coordinates. Under the Brier loss, with both
labels at every $x$ and the all-missing mask present, identification then
holds iff $\delta$ is constant on incomplete inputs. The oracle is strictly
more informative than C. In the FR1 analysis, which assumed $w$ known and no
labels at all, the analogous condition was that $\delta$ is constant on
\emph{all} inputs (\cref{app:fr1}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Support restriction versus $\Gamma\to\infty$]\label{prop:support}
If $p^*(c,r)>0$ for some incomplete $r$ and a cell with $\kappa_c=0$, then
$p^*\notin\Mcc(\Gamma)$ for every $\Gamma$ and also $p^*\notin$
\textsc{Support-only}. Only \textsc{No-model} contains $p^*$. Increasing
$\Gamma$ never repairs a positivity violation.
\end{proposition}

Proofs are in \cref{app:proofs}. They are elementary and have been checked
only by the authors; the exact certificates attached to each linear program
verify individual instances and are not proofs of these statements.

\section{Counterexamples and Failure Modes}
\label{sec:counter}

\begin{example}[Identical unlabelled data, opposite rankings; $w$ known]\label{ex:cx1}
Take $d=1$, $P(X=1)=1/2$, $P(Y=1\mid X=x)=1/4$ or $3/4$, and random dropout rate
$1/4$. Let $f_A$ be Bayes under MCAR ($f_A(\varnothing)=1/2$) and $f_B$ impute
the mode ($f_B(\varnothing)=1/4$). Then $D=0$ on complete inputs,
$D((x,0),\varnothing)=3/16$ and $D((x,1),\varnothing)=-5/16$. Among policies
that reproduce the MCAR unlabelled law, the risk difference ranges over
exactly $[-5/64,3/64]$, and random dropout gives $-1/64$: both rankings occur
with identical unlabelled data.
\end{example}

\textbf{Complete-case dropout can rank the models the wrong way.} In our
population (\cref{sec:exp}), environment E2m makes modality 2 more likely to
be missing when $Y=0$, with the same full-data law and mask frequencies as
MCAR. Its natural-missingness difference is $\Dnat=+0.0075$ (model B better),
while random dropout on complete cases gives $-0.0436$ (A better) and random
dropout on the population gives $-0.0253$ (\cref{tab:truths}). The reversal is
one hand-specified case, not a frequency.

\textbf{A too-small $\Gamma$ can certify the wrong sign.} For E2m the true
parameter is $\Gamma_{cc}\approx 2.32$. In setting C with $\Gamma=3/2$ the
feasible set is non-empty and its interval $[-0.0495,-0.0239]$ certifies ``A
better''. With the oracle information and $\Gamma=2$ the interval
$[0.0018,0.0025]$ has the correct sign but excludes the truth. Feasibility
therefore does not validate $\Gamma$; non-emptiness is only a necessary
condition (\cref{app:fr2}).

\textbf{Support.} In a constructed check, one cell is never fully observed.
Under every $\Mcc(\Gamma)$, including $\Gamma=1000$, and under
\textsc{Support-only}, the oracle setting becomes infeasible. In setting C
these sets stay feasible and contain the true value although the true law is
excluded. \textsc{No-model} contains it in both settings (\cref{tab:support}).

\section{Sampling Uncertainty}
\label{sec:sampling}

The intervals above are population identified sets. With a finite sample,
the plug-in interval (sample frequencies treated as exact) is an estimate,
not a confidence set. In the FR1 analysis the plug-in interval contained the
population interval in only 8/100 ($n=100$) and 5/100 ($n=1000$) replications.
Those numbers are containment frequencies of an estimate, not coverage of a
confidence procedure.

\textbf{Common confidence region.} All settings are functions of one
observed-data law $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (here the multinomial of observable
cells). For each $\Gamma$ let $\mathcal{I}(\Gamma;P)$ be the identified set of
$p$ given $P_{\mathrm{obs}}=P$, and let $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma;\mathcal{R})$ be a
set with $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma;\mathcal{R})\supseteq\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{I}(\Gamma;P)$
for a confidence region $\mathcal{R}$. On the single event
$\{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathcal{R}\}$, $\mathcal{I}(\Gamma;P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma;\mathcal{R})$ for every $\Gamma$ at once; this is the whole
coverage argument. It concerns identified sets: it does \emph{not} validate
the sensitivity assumption. If $\Mcc(\Gamma)$ is false, $\mathcal{I}$ may omit
the truth or be empty, and the interval can miss $\Dnat$ with any
probability. The only data-based statement about $\Gamma$ is one-sided:
if $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma;\mathcal{R})=\emptyset$, then $\Mcc(\Gamma)$ is rejected
at level $\alpha$ (under a correct $\Mcc(\Gamma)$ and $P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathcal{R}$
the true law is feasible). Non-emptiness is not evidence for $\Gamma$.

\begin{proposition}[Outer confidence interval]\label{prop:outer}
In setting C, let the $K$ observable cell probabilities
$(\theta_c,\omega(r,o))$ be estimated from $n$ i.i.d.\ units, and let
$B_\alpha$ be the box $|\hat\vartheta_k-\vartheta_k|\le\varepsilon$ with
$\varepsilon=\sqrt{\log(2K/\alpha)/(2n)}$ (Hoeffding with a Bonferroni
split), rounded outward. Relax \eqref{eq:mcc-lin} to
$b_r\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c/\Gamma\le p(c,r)\le\Gamma b_r\kappa^{\mathrm{hi}}_c$
and bound $b_r\le\Gamma/\sum_c\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c$. On the event that
$B_\alpha$ contains the true probabilities, which has probability at least
$1-\alpha$, the relaxed program contains every point of the population
identified set. Its interval therefore covers the identified interval, and
hence $\Dnat$, with probability at least $1-\alpha$, simultaneously for all
$\Gamma$.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Zeros and stratum sizes.} The box is built on \emph{joint} cell
probabilities with the fixed total $n$, so the random numbers of complete and
incomplete units (random stratum sizes) need no conditioning. An empirical
zero ($\hat\theta_c=0$) gets $\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c=0<\kappa^{\mathrm{hi}}_c$ and is
\emph{not} turned into a structural zero; if all $\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c=0$
(e.g.\ $n_{\mathrm{complete}}=0$) the relaxed set reduces exactly to the support
restriction $\{p(c,r)=0:\kappa^{\mathrm{hi}}_c=0\}$ (v1 code raised an error
here). The \emph{plug-in} program, by contrast, treats $\hat\theta_c=0$ as
structural and is undefined when $n_{\mathrm{complete}}=0$; it is an estimate,
never a confidence statement.

The construction is conservative. It is not sharp, and we have not analysed
its width or compared it with the procedures of
\citet{imbens2004confidence,stoye2009more}. Validity needs i.i.d.\ sampling,
\cref{ass:labels}, and a correctly specified $\Mcc(\Gamma)$.

\section{Experiments: One Finite Population}
\label{sec:exp}

\textbf{Design.} Everything is exact (rational arithmetic, a bounded-variable
simplex with primal--dual and Farkas certificates). There is one population:
$d=2$; $P(x)=(2/5,3/20,3/20,3/10)$ for $x\in\{00,01,10,11\}$;
$P(Y=1\mid x)=(1/10,3/5,2/5,9/10)$. Model A is the Bayes predictor under
MCAR dropout, an idealised dropout-trained model. Model B imputes the mode and
applies the full-data Bayes predictor. The dropout law $q$ drops modalities
independently with rates $1/5$ and $3/10$. The environments are

correlated MCAR. The population was fixed before any analysis, but results
from it were seen during development (a smoke test preceded the first
pre-registered run), so all experiments are \textbf{exploratory}. No other
population was searched. Runs: P2-FR1 (9.9\,s) and P2-FR2 (33.3\,s), both
on 2 CPU threads.

\subsection{Semi-synthetic pilot on public data (P2-PILOT1)}
\label{sec:pilot}

\textbf{Design, fixed before the data were read.} We use the UCI Adult census
data \cite{becker1996adult} (CC BY 4.0; 48{,}842 rows). The configuration was
committed before download: the entity is the tuple of all attributes except
the sampling weight (44{,}355 entities, so duplicates never cross the split),
and entities are split 50/50 by a hash (22{,}080 train, 22{,}275 evaluation).
There are two ``modalities'', each coarsened by fixed, a-priori thresholds to
three levels: education years ($\le9$, $10$--$12$, $\ge13$) and weekly hours
($\le34$, $35$--$45$, $>45$). $Y$ is income $>50$K. Models A and B are defined as
in \cref{sec:exp}, fitted on the training split with Laplace smoothing and
using the binned features only. Natural masks on the evaluation split follow
two fixed rules: modality 1 is missing with probability $0.15$; modality 2 is
missing with probability $0.15+0.25\,Y+0.10\,\mathbb{1}[\text{hours}>45]$
(primary, label- and value-dependent) or $0.30$ (control, MCAR). The seed is
fixed. The evaluator's dropout law is independent with rates $(0.15,0.30)$.
Labels of incomplete units are hidden from every method and used only for the
held-out difference. The masks are synthetic and the covariates and labels are
real, so this is not an analysis of natural modality missingness.

\textbf{Is $D(c,r)$ a fixed loss after coarsening?} Only if the predictors
depend on the data through the coarse cell and the mask. If they used the fine
covariates, the loss inside a coarse cell would vary, and its average could
depend on $R$ (missingness within a cell). $D$ would then not be fixed, and one
would need either a different estimand or an explicit outer bound replacing
$D$ by $[\min,\max]$ over the fine points in each cell. Our predictors use the
binned inputs only. The run recomputes every unit's loss and checks that it
equals $D(c,r)$ of its cell (PASS). The estimand is therefore the
natural-missingness Brier difference of the \emph{binned} predictors.

\textbf{Two populations.} The evaluation split with its realised masks is
treated as a finite population. Its observable law is known exactly, so the
setting-C intervals are its exact identified sets. The outer confidence
intervals instead treat the evaluation units as an i.i.d.\ sample. Note that
the realised finite population has $\Gamma_{cc}=2.85$ under the MNAR rule but
also $1.79$ under the MCAR control: random mask draws in small cells spread
the ratios. A finite population's $\Gamma_{cc}$ therefore mixes mechanism and
sampling variation, and $\Gamma_{cc}=1$ is empty even for the control.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One population.} All numbers come from one binary population
with two modalities, two fixed models and the Brier loss. They show that the
stated phenomena can occur; they say nothing about how often they occur. The
population was seen during development.
\item \textbf{Real data.} The pilot uses real covariates and labels from one
census dataset but synthetic masks, two coarse ``modalities'' and one split.
Results on real multimodal missingness are not reported
\todo{P2-real: natural-missingness dataset, labels only for complete cases,
approval and license}. Continuous or high-dimensional features would require
discretisation or smoothness assumptions. The resulting bounds would be
estimates, not population sharp bounds.
\item \textbf{Assumptions.} $\Mcc(\Gamma)$ is one of many sensitivity models.
$\Gamma$ is not estimable, and a feasible but misspecified $\Gamma$ can
certify the wrong sign (\cref{sec:counter}). \Cref{ass:labels} excludes
labels on incomplete units, and the case of a label indicator independent of
the mask is not analysed \todo{P2-S: label availability not tied to the mask}.
\item \textbf{Inference.} The outer confidence interval is conservative and
its width is not analysed. Sharper procedures
\cite{imbens2004confidence,stoye2009more} are not implemented
\todo{P2-FR3: valid and less conservative inference; coverage study}.
\item \textbf{Proofs and novelty.} The proofs are elementary and self-checked
only. Several ingredients are known (see \cref{sec:related}), and a
forward-citation search of the closest works has not been done
\todo{RW: forward citations of Subbaswamy et al.\ 2021, Guerdan et al.\ 2024,
Zhou et al.\ 2023, Choi et al.\ 2026}.
\item \textbf{Losses.} Only the Brier loss was computed; 0--1 and log loss are
not run \todo{P2-FR1-loss}.
\end{itemize}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A missing-modality evaluation identifies little about deployment rankings
unless the evaluator also knows how often each modality is missing and what
the incomplete units look like. Even then, with labels only on complete
cases, the natural-missingness Brier difference is identified only when the
two models agree on every incomplete input. Beyond that point, conclusions
depend on an unverifiable sensitivity parameter, and in our population they
are lost at modest values. In the public-data pilot, the observable
information never determined the ranking, although complete-case dropout
reported one. Treating complete cases as the population (the
oracle setting) can make conclusions look far more robust than the observable
information supports. Plug-in feasibility checks and plug-in intervals are
not confidence statements. We recommend that missing-modality benchmarks
report the mask frequencies and the unlabelled law of incomplete units, and
present robustness as a function of an explicit sensitivity parameter
rather than as a single dropout score.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Impact Statement}
This paper concerns the evaluation of predictive models when inputs are
missing for informative reasons, a common situation in clinical and other
high-stakes settings. Its main message is cautionary: standard
random-dropout evaluations can rank models incorrectly and should not be read
as evidence of safety under natural missingness. The analysis uses only
synthetic, hand-specified distributions. No personal or patient data were
used, and no model was trained. A possible misuse is to present a narrow
interval computed under an optimistic sensitivity parameter as a
certification. We explicitly show that this can certify the wrong ranking.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\textbf{\Cref{lem:linear} (exact linearisation; closure in B/D).} Fix $r$.
If $\rho_r>0$ and $(p,\lambda_r)$ satisfy \eqref{eq:mcc} multiplied by
$\rho_r$, then $b_r=\rho_r\lambda_r$ satisfies \eqref{eq:mcc-lin} (with
$\kappa=v$; with $\kappa=\theta=\rho_{\one}v$ use
$b_r=\rho_r\lambda_r/\rho_{\one}$). Conversely, given $(p,b_r)$ with
$\rho_r=\sum_c p(c,r)>0$, set $\lambda_r=b_r/\rho_r$ (resp.\
$b_r\rho_{\one}/\rho_r$). If $\rho_r=0$ both descriptions force $p(\cdot,r)=0$.
$\lambda_r$ enters no other row. In B and D the rows
$p(c,\one)=\rho_{\one}v_c$ allow $\rho_{\one}=0$, under which $v$ is not the
complete-case law of any population. Any such feasible point is a limit of
valid points: set $\rho_{\one}=\epsilon$, scale all incomplete masses by
$1-\epsilon$, and scale $b$ accordingly. The LP interval is therefore the
closure of the identified interval. The endpoint
$\min_r\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r$ in \cref{prop:gamma1}(ii) is of this kind when
it requires $\rho_{\one}=0$. $\square$

\textbf{\Cref{thm:general}.} If $g\perp(z-z')$ for all $z,z'\in F$, then
$g^\top z$ is constant. Conversely, if $g^\top z\neq g^\top z'$ for some
$z,z'$, then $g\not\perp z-z'$. For a polyhedron
$\{Az=b,\ l\le z\le u\}$, $\mathrm{aff}(F)=\{Az=b,\ z_j=\mathrm{const}\ (j\in T)\}$,
where $T$ is the set of implicitly tight bounds. So the span of differences
is the null space of $A$ augmented with $\{e_j\}_{j\in T}$. Adding $e_j$ for
all constant coordinates (a superset of $T$ whose extra rows lie in the
row space already) does not change it. $\square$

\textbf{\Cref{prop:gamma1}.} At $\Gamma=1$, \eqref{eq:mcc-lin} gives
$p(c,r)=b_r\kappa_c$, so $P(C\mid R=r)\propto\kappa\propto v$. Then
$\Dnat=\sum_r\rho_r\sum_c v_cD(c,r)$. In C, $\rho$ is fixed by the rows, so
$\Dnat$ is identified. Feasibility requires the stated equality of the
observed and implied unlabelled laws. In B there is no row on $\rho$ other than
the simplex. In D the $\mu$-rows are satisfied by $\rho_r=b_r$ whenever the
implied conditionals match, and add no restriction on $\rho$. The range over
the simplex with $\rho_{\one}>0$ is the stated set, and its closure is the
hull of the $\Delta^{\mathrm{cc}}_r$. (iii) is immediate. $\square$

\textbf{\Cref{prop:C}.} Take the lifted polytope in $(p,b)$; in C the rows
fix $p(c,\one)$, the group sums $\sum_{c\in G(r,o)}p(c,r)$, and hence the
total mass. Because $p^*$ satisfies \eqref{eq:mcc-lin} strictly for some
$b^*$ (the true ratio spread is strictly below $\Gamma^2$ and $\theta_c>0$),
every direction $h$ with $h(c,\one)=0$ and
$\sum_{c\in G(r,o)}h(c,r)=0$, together with $\Delta b=0$, keeps
$p^*\pm th$ feasible for small $t$. Directions that move only $b$ leave $p$,
and hence $\Dnat$, unchanged. So $\Dnat$ is constant iff $D(\cdot,r)$ is
orthogonal to every zero-sum vector on each group, i.e.\ constant on each
group. Under the Brier loss, for $c,c'\in G(r,o)$ with labels $0$ and $1$,
$D(c,r)-D(c',r)=2\delta(o,r)$, so constancy forces $\delta(o,r)=0$, and then
$u(o,r)=0$ as well. Conversely, if $\delta=0$ on all incomplete inputs, then
$D(c,r)=0$ for $r\neq\one$ and $\Dnat=\sum_c\theta_cD(c,\one)$. $\square$

\textbf{Remark (oracle A).} The additional rows $\sum_rp(c,r)=w_c$ add the
functions $a(c)$. Differencing labels at fixed $x$ gives
$-2\delta(x_r,r)=a(x,1)-a(x,0)$ for every incomplete $r$. With the all-missing
mask present, $\delta(\varnothing)=\alpha(x)$ for every $x$, so $\delta$ is
constant on incomplete inputs. The converse holds with $a(x,y)=-2\kappa y$.
$\square$

\textbf{\Cref{prop:support}.} Every $\Mcc(\Gamma)$ and \textsc{Support-only}
impose $p(c,r)\le\Gamma b_r\kappa_c=0$ when $\kappa_c=0$. $\square$

\textbf{\Cref{prop:outer}.} Hoeffding's inequality for each cell indicator,
followed by a union bound over $K$ cells, gives
$P(\vartheta\in B_\alpha)\ge1-\alpha$. Outward rounding only enlarges
$B_\alpha$. On this event, any $p$ in the population identified set has
$p(c,\one)=\theta_c\in[\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c,\kappa^{\mathrm{hi}}_c]$ and group
sums inside the box. With $b_r$ its population scale,
$p(c,r)\ge b_r\theta_c/\Gamma\ge b_r\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c/\Gamma$ and
$p(c,r)\le\Gamma b_r\kappa^{\mathrm{hi}}_c$. Summing the lower inequality over
$c$ gives $b_r\le\Gamma/\sum_c\theta_c\le\Gamma/\sum_c\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c$,
so the auxiliary bounds are not active. Hence $p$ is feasible for the relaxed
program. The event does not involve $\Gamma$, so the statement holds for all
$\Gamma$ simultaneously. $\square$

\textbf{Status.} All proofs above are complete as written but have only been
self-checked. Open: whether the closure caveat can be removed by a
constraint $\rho_{\one}\ge\underline\rho$ that is known in practice (UNPROVED);
width guarantees for \cref{prop:outer} (UNPROVED); extension to label
availability independent of the mask (NOT STARTED).

\section{Linear-Programming Formulation and Checks (P2-FR2)}
\label{app:fr2}

The program uses the substitution $p(c,r)=b_r\kappa_c/\Gamma+s(c,r)$,
$s(c,r)+t(c,r)=(\Gamma\kappa^{\mathrm{hi}}_c-\kappa^{\mathrm{lo}}_c/\Gamma)b_r$,
with $s,t\ge0$ and finite bounds on all auxiliaries (\texttt{src/msid/observation.py}).
Every endpoint carries a certificate that is checked independently of the
pivoting code: either a primal point with exact feasibility and a dual vector
whose weak-duality bound equals the objective, or a Farkas vector. The
following engineering checks all passed (P2-FR2 manifest):
\begin{itemize}
\item every linear program was certified (0 uncertified);
\item nesting in information (A $\subseteq$ C $\subseteq$ D $\subseteq$ B) and in $\Gamma$;
\item whenever the truth satisfies $\Mcc(\Gamma)$, the truth lies in the interval;
\item the structural-zero check (\cref{prop:support});
\item the outer interval contains the population interval whenever the box
contains the truth;
\item the general criterion (\cref{thm:general}) agrees with zero width in
all audited cases.
\end{itemize}

\section{Earlier Oracle Analysis (P2-FR1)}
