\begin{abstract}
Multimodal models are routinely compared by deleting modalities at random.
We ask for which natural missingness policies such a ranking remains valid.
For finite supports we show that the set of deployment risk differences
compatible with a bounded-selection sensitivity model and the available
marginal information is an interval with attainable endpoints (two linear
programs), that only the cell-by-mask interaction of the loss difference can
move it, and that unlabelled deployment data can refute small sensitivity
parameters but never identify label-dependent missingness.

\section{Introduction}
Random modality dropout (artificial mask $M \perp (X,Y)$) vs.\ natural
missingness (mask $R \sim \pi(\cdot\mid x,y)$). Question: range of $\pi$ over
which the sign of $\Delta(\pi)=R_A(\pi)-R_B(\pi)$ equals that of $\Delta(q)$.
Contributions (to be confirmed against related work \todo{RELATED_WORK verification}):
(i) exact sharp interval relative to stated assumptions;
(ii) row-space characterisation of point identification (only the interaction matters);
(iii) ranking-transfer threshold $\Gamma^*$ and a closed-form certificate;
(iv) falsification of small $\Gamma$ from unlabelled deployment data.
We make no claim about how often rankings reverse in practice.

\section{Setting}
Cells $c=(x,y)$, masks $r$, full-data law $w$, fixed predictors $f_A,f_B$,
mask-specific losses $L_f(c,r)$, $D=L_A-L_B$,
$\Delta(\pi)=\sum_{c,r} w_c\,\pi(r\mid c)\,D(c,r)$.
Policy sets $\Pi(\Gamma;K)$: $\Gamma$-box around the dropout law $q$ plus
$K\in\{\text{none},\text{feature},\text{pattern},\text{observed law}\}$.
Assumptions A1--A4 (known full-data law, $\Gamma$-box, exact marginals, fixed models).
Distinctions: natural $R$ vs.\ artificial $M$; population $w$ vs.\ complete sample
$\hat w$ vs.\ complete-case law; exact constraints vs.\ plug-in; oracle vs.\ available.
